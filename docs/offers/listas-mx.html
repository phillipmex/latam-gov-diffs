What you get

An invite to a private GitHub repository that carries the same nightly snapshot and diff stream for the 69-B list. A repository, not an endpoint.

  • The diff twelve hours early. The harvest runs at 06:15 UTC and commits to your private repository in the same run; the public archive is pushed at 18:15 UTC. That is a head start on this repository's publication, not on SAT's - SAT publishes to everyone at once.
  • A dated copy of the list, every night, kept for good. SAT overwrites this file in place. Every night that is not archived is gone, from SAT's site and from everywhere else, and no amount of money spent later brings it back.
  • Movement between stages read as a change, not as a deletion. Rows are keyed on rfc plus numero_y_fecha_de_oficio_global_de_presuncion_sat - the proceeding, not the stage. A taxpayer going from Presunto to Definitivo is the most valuable event in this feed, and it arrives as one changed record with the before and the after on it.
  • An alert that comes to you. Watch the repository and GitHub emails you, or pushes to your phone, on any night that produced a change. A night with no change produces no commit and no noise.
  • A changes.json written for machines. One file to poll: the latest version pair, the added / changed / removed counts, and the path to the full diff. Compare one field with the id you last processed and stop if they match.
  • Somebody to ask. Open an issue in your own repository; first response within two business days.

What is not included

  • No history before 2026-09-07. There is none, anywhere, including at SAT. See below - it is the first thing on this page for a reason.
  • No hosted API. No endpoint, no key, no query language. You clone a git repository.
  • No webhooks. Nothing calls your server; GitHub notifications and changes.json are the two ways you find out.
  • No SLA. Best effort, nightly, on free hosted runners. If SAT is down that night produces nothing.
  • No legal opinion, and no substitute for SAT's own constancia. This is a record of what was published, and nothing more.

The point-in-time attestation - $250

One written statement about one RFC on one date, with the evidence attached.

It answers the question SAT's own site cannot: what did the 69-B list say on a particular day? SAT publishes the current list and nothing else, so the only way anyone can answer that is to have taken a copy on that day. This archive did, and does, every night.

What the document contains

  • The answer, in one line. Whether the RFC appeared on the list on a given date, or across two dates if you ask for a span - answered as Yes, throughout, Yes, in part or No, never collapsed to a single row.
  • The situacion del contribuyente it carried - Presunto, Desvirtuado, Definitivo or Sentencia Favorable - and, if it moved inside the span, where it moved.
  • Every oficio number and publication date the list recorded for that proceeding, SAT-side and DOF-side, exactly as SAT wrote them. An RFC that appears in two unrelated proceedings gets both records; the document never silently picks one.
  • The evidence behind the answer. The snapshot version ids, their sha256 - the hash of the bytes SAT actually served - the SAT document URL, the Last-Modified SAT reported at the time, the observation timestamps on both sides, and the next observation, so the edge of the evidence is named rather than implied.
  • Delivered as Markdown, laid out so it prints straight to PDF from any browser or editor - into your private repository if you already have a listas-mx subscription, or as a GitHub gist link if you do not. Turnaround: 3 business days from payment and from your telling us the RFC and the date.

## What this archive can and cannot reach

SAT publishes the 69-B list as a single file that is overwritten in place. There
is no dated back-series, no versioned URL and no public history, so a statement
about any day can only be made by somebody who took a copy on that day.

- **Coverage begins 2026-09-07**, the day this archive took its first snapshot.
  No date before that can be attested here, and it cannot be recovered from SAT
  either.
- **Coverage ends 2026-09-07**, the last day the archive observed the source.
- Between two observations the list is taken to be unchanged. Each evidence
  block names the next observation, so the exact edge of the evidence is
  visible.
- 91 rows in the published list have their RFC suppressed to `XXXXXXXXXXXX` by
  court order. A search for a real RFC never matches those rows.

How delivery works

  1. You pay, and give a GitHub username. A private repository is created for you and seeded with the whole sat69b archive: every stored snapshot, every diff, and changes.json.
  2. GitHub sends you the collaborator invite. There is no message from us, no onboarding call, and no address to hand over.

Questions

Can I get this history somewhere else?

No, and that is the whole point of this feed. Of the three publishers in this archive, SAT is the only one that keeps no back versions of this file: the NF-e portal lists every dated cClassTrib release, and SAT's own Anexo 20 workbooks stay reachable at their old URLs even when nothing links to them - but the 69-B list is one filename, overwritten in place, with no dated copy anywhere. Whatever it said last March is gone. What is being sold is not a history nobody has; it is the alert on the next change, and a dated record from 2026-09-07 onward that will be a history in a year.

Who is this for?

Compliance and accounts-payable teams who screen suppliers against 69-B, and the developers who automate that screening. If a supplier you paid is later published as Definitivo, the invoices you booked from that RFC lose their tax effect, and the question that follows is always the same: what did the list say on the day we checked? Without a dated copy there is no answer to that question.

There are no diffs yet. What am I actually paying for on day one?

A dated snapshot every night, an alert on the first change, and the ability to have a point-in-time statement issued about any date from 2026-09-07 onward. That is the honest list. The Last-Modified on the file has read 22 January 2026 since the first probe, so no comparison exists yet - and when it moves, this will be the first record-level diff this list has ever produced anywhere. If a feed whose value accrues rather than arriving on day one is not what you want, the free viewer shows the same snapshot and costs nothing.

Is the attestation the same as SAT's constancia?

No, and it is not offered as one. SAT's constancia is SAT's own statement about its own live records. This is a third party's evidence of what a public SAT file said on a given date, with the sha256 of the bytes and the Last-Modified SAT reported at the time, so somebody else can check it. It is not legal advice and it says nothing about the validity of any invoice or contract. Where a proceeding is disputed, it is one exhibit among others and your lawyer's problem, not a substitute for one.

What happens if SAT changes the file format?

That night's harvest produces nothing rather than producing something wrong, and the issue tracker says so. The parser is fixed as fast as it can be fixed and the stored file is re-parsed, so nothing already archived is lost. There is no SLA and no credit: the honest description is best effort, nightly. The failure that matters more is a night SAT is unreachable, because that night's version of the list can never be recovered - and the archive will say plainly which nights those were.

What happens when I cancel?

Access runs to the end of the period you have paid for, then the collaborator invite is revoked and the private repository is archived. Nothing is clawed back: anything you cloned stays cloned, and every snapshot you were sent is still yours. The public archive keeps publishing, free, twelve hours behind.

Why should I pay when the archive is free?

Often you should not, and the free tier is genuinely the right answer for most readers. Three things are behind the price and nothing else: the diff twelve hours earlier, an alert that reaches you instead of you remembering to look, and somebody to ask. The attestation adds a fourth: somebody who signs the answer. If none of those is worth $99 a month, use the free viewer - it is not a trial and it is not reduced.
